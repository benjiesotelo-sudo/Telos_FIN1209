# Can a student answer every check from the slides alone?

Audit of all 25 checks and all 50 items in the Chapter 1 deck, 2 September 2026,
against the 227 slide teaching edition.

**The test applied.** For each item: could a student who sat through the session
and saw only what was on screen answer it? A fact that lives only in the speaker
notes, only in the lecture notes, or only in the textbook does **not** count.
The speaker notes fail the test twice over: the student edition carries none of
them at all, and nothing in the room guarantees the instructor said them.

**The result.** 48 of 50 items passed. Two failed, both have been fixed, and
nothing was deleted or weakened. The two fixes are described at the end.

Slide numbers below are from the current build. They are not typed anywhere in
the plan or the notes, which resolve them from the deck, so they move on their
own when the deck changes; treat the numbers here as a snapshot and the slide
titles as the durable reference.

## The two fixes

### Fix 1. Check 8 now comes after the two slides it examines

Item 8.2 asks the room to judge the statement *"A technical analyst usually
cannot determine intrinsic value."* That sentence is on the slide **The
technical analyst, in four lines**, and that slide used to come **after** the
check. The other three statements were all teachable from earlier slides, and
the two options that survive elimination differ by exactly that statement, so a
student who had not seen it had no basis to choose between them.

**Fixed by moving the check, not by touching the question.** Check 8 now sits
immediately after *The fundamentalist, in four lines* and *The technical
analyst, in four lines*, which is also the better teaching order: the check
tests timing and the two profiles, and now both have been taught. No content
was invented and no slide changed.

### Fix 2. The Random walk slide now names the Markovian condition

Item 20.1 asks what **the Markovian condition** states. The phrase appeared
nowhere on any slide the student had seen. It was in the speaker note for the
Random walk slide, which told the instructor to say it aloud, and it is in the
lecture notes, and it is on the Part 5 recap, which comes after the check. The
student edition carries no speaker notes at all, so a student revising from
their own deck would never meet the phrase.

**Fixed by putting the fact on the slide that should have carried it.** The
plain words row of the Random walk term slide now reads:

> The claim that prices move purely by chance, so nothing that happened before
> tells you anything. Its second half, that current price has no influence over
> future price, is the Markovian condition.

This teaches only what the textbook teaches: the formal row already carried both
clauses of the definition, and the naming follows the lecture notes, which are
written from the book. The speaker note changed from supplying the fact to
pointing at it.
