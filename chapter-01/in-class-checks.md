## Part 1 of 6 - Why Anybody Analyzes a Market

### Check 1 - Instincts, price, and the basic rule

**Q1.** Which instinct does the text treat as the strongest driver of market behavior?

- A. The instinct for comfort
- B. The instinct to propagate
- **C. The instinct to survive**
- D. The instinct to compete

Answer: **C**. Survival almost always precedes the need for comfort or to propagate.

**Q2.** Price is described as a variable of change mainly because:

- **A. It moves, and everyone can observe it**
- B. It is set by the company's accountants
- C. It is fixed for the trading day
- D. It equals the intrinsic value of the asset

Answer: **A**. Price is popular precisely because it is both changing and openly observable.


### Check 2 - The chart and the two jobs

**Q1.** A price-time chart tracks which two dimensions?

- **A. Price and time**
- B. Price and volume
- C. Volume and open interest
- D. Value and time

Answer: **A**. Price sits on the vertical axis and time on the horizontal axis.

**Q2.** Recording that a stock's widest daily range last year was 8 pesos is an example of:

- A. Forecasting
- B. Intrinsic valuation
- **C. Identification**
- D. Sentiment analysis

Answer: **C**. Describing what already happened is the identification function, with no claim about the future.


### Check 3 - The four words for a trade

**Q1.** Selling to close a position you already held is called:

- **A. Liquidating**
- B. Going short
- C. Covering
- D. Going long

Answer: **A**. Liquidating is selling to close; covering is buying to close a short.

**Q2.** A trader borrows shares and sells them at 90 pesos. Two weeks later they buy them back at 74 pesos. The second action is:

- A. Going long
- **B. Covering**
- C. Liquidating
- D. Scaling in

Answer: **B**. Buying to close a previously shorted position is covering.


## Part 2 of 6 - Three Ways to Forecast a Price

### Check 4 - Value and the three approaches

**Q1.** A share trades at 45 pesos and its estimated intrinsic value is 60 pesos. The share is:

- A. Overvalued, and supply should be generated
- **B. Undervalued, and demand should be generated**
- C. Fairly valued
- D. Overbought

Answer: **B**. Price below estimated intrinsic value means undervalued, which potentially generates demand.

**Q2.** Select the accurate statements. I. Fundamental analysis estimates intrinsic value. II. Technical analysis asks what a business is worth. III. Information analysis draws on public sources such as news reports. IV. All three try to forecast future price.

- **A. Only I, III, and IV are correct**
- B. Only I and II are correct
- C. Only II and III are correct
- D. All are correct

Answer: **A**. Statement II is false: valuing the business is the fundamentalist's job, not the technician's.


### Check 5 - Approaches and their limits

**Q1.** An analyst first identifies a strengthening industry, then screens for the best company inside it. This is:

- A. A bottom-up approach
- **B. A top-down approach**
- C. A contrarian approach
- D. Information analysis

Answer: **B**. Starting from broad market or sector conditions and narrowing down is top-down.

**Q2.** The main weakness of fundamental analysis, for a trader, is that it:

- A. Cannot value a company
- B. Ignores a company's debt
- **C. Cannot provide clear short term price and time levels to act on**
- D. Requires a chart

Answer: **C**. Its stated main weakness is the inability to give specific short term price levels or timing.


### Check 6 - Information, and the first definition

**Q1.** Why is publicly available information usually of limited forecasting value?

- **A. Those with non-public material information have already moved the market**
- B. It is difficult to find
- C. It is always inaccurate
- D. It cannot be plotted on a chart

Answer: **A**. By the time news is public, the substantial move has usually already happened.

**Q2.** Murphy's definition identifies the main tool of technical analysis as:

- A. Financial statements
- B. Polls and surveys
- C. Regression models
- **D. Charts**

Answer: **D**. Murphy defines it as the study of market action primarily through the use of charts.


### Check 7 - Three definitions in a row

**Q1.** Which definition draws the line between studying the market and studying the goods the market deals in?

- A. Murphy
- B. Pring
- C. Lim
- **D. Edwards and Magee**

Answer: **D**. That line is Edwards and Magee's, from Technical Analysis of Stock Trends.

**Q2.** Pring describes the art of technical analysis as identifying:

- A. The intrinsic value of a stock
- B. The exact top of a market
- **C. A trend reversal at a relatively early stage**
- D. The cause of a price move

Answer: **C**. Pring's phrasing is to identify a trend reversal at a relatively early stage and ride the trend.


### Check 8 - Timing, and who answers which question

**Q1.** Which is offered by technically based market timing but not by fundamentally based market timing?

- A. Identifying an undervalued stock
- B. An estimate of intrinsic value
- **C. A precise price and time of entry**
- D. A view on a company's debt level

Answer: **C**. Precise entry and exit price and time is exactly what fundamental timing lacks.

**Q2.** Pick the accurate statements. I. A technical analyst is more concerned with effects than causes. II. A fundamentalist is mainly concerned with intrinsic value. III. A technical analyst usually cannot determine intrinsic value. IV. A fundamentalist can tell you the most advantageous moment to enter.

- **A. Only I, II, and III are correct**
- B. Only I and II are correct
- C. Only II and IV are correct
- D. All are correct

Answer: **A**. Statement IV is false: the fundamentalist can tell you which company, not the best moment.


### Check 9 - Market action and its data

**Q1.** Flow of funds analysis involves the study of:

- A. Oscillators
- B. Linear regression
- C. Point and figure charts
- **D. Margin debt**

Answer: **D**. Margin debt is a flow of funds measure; the other three are chart or statistical studies.

**Q2.** Of all the data technical analysts use, the most important is:

- A. Open interest
- B. Sentiment
- **C. Price**
- D. Market breadth

Answer: **C**. Price is the most important, followed closely by volume action.


## Part 3 of 6 - Classifying Technical Analysis

### Check 10 - The four branches

**Q1.** Studying skewness, regression and volatility of returns belongs to which branch?

- **A. Statistical**
- B. Classical
- C. Sentiment
- D. Behavioral

Answer: **A**. Those are quantitative measures, which is the statistical branch.

**Q2.** Japanese candlestick patterns and overlay indicators belong to:

- **A. Classical technical analysis**
- B. Statistical analysis
- C. Sentiment analysis
- D. Behavioral analysis

Answer: **A**. Candlesticks, oscillators and overlays are the conventional, classical toolkit.


### Check 11 - The two approaches

**Q1.** A trader who buys at support, shorts at resistance and prefers limit entry orders is following:

- A. A momentum seeking approach
- **B. A mean reverting approach**
- C. An arbitrage approach
- D. A random walk approach

Answer: **B**. Buying support, shorting resistance and using limit orders is the contrarian, mean reverting profile.

**Q2.** Pick the accurate statements. I. A momentum seeking approach is usually trend based. II. Darvas Box trading is an example of a momentum seeking approach. III. Contrarians expect price to be more mean reverting. IV. Contrarians look for impending reversals.

- A. Only I is correct
- B. Only I, II, and IV are correct
- C. Only I and IV are correct
- **D. All are correct**

Answer: **D**. All four statements are accurate descriptions of the two approaches.


### Check 12 - Strengths, weaknesses, and the prophecy

**Q1.** A random walk view of the market implies that:

- A. Technical analysis is still useful because patterns may arise by chance
- B. Technical analysis is unreliable only in volatile markets
- C. Technical analysis works only on long timeframes
- **D. Technical analysis is pointless because chart patterns arise out of pure chance**

Answer: **D**. If all action is random, patterns carry no information and analysis is pointless.

**Q2.** In the self-fulfilling prophecy cycle, participants preempting each other for better fills happens at:

- A. Stage 1
- B. Stage 2
- **C. Stage 4**
- D. Stage 6

Answer: **C**. Preempting is stage 4, and it is what erodes the reliability of the signal.


## Part 4 of 6 - Subjectivity

### Check 13 - Where subjectivity lives

**Q1.** Which part of technical analysis is described as objective?

- A. The interpretation of indicators
- **B. The chart as a historical record of price and market action**
- C. The inference of future price
- D. The choice of which indicator to apply

Answer: **B**. The chart is an objective record; everything done to it afterwards is subjective.

**Q2.** Analyzing price and market action consists of which three activities?

- A. Buying, holding, selling
- B. Recording, charting, publishing
- C. Screening, valuing, timing
- **D. Identifying, interpreting, inferring**

Answer: **D**. The three activities are identifying patterns, interpreting the data, and inferring future behavior.


### Check 14 - Three kinds of signal

**Q1.** A 20 period oscillator reads overbought while a 100 period oscillator on the same chart reads oversold. This is best described as:

- A. A contradiction that invalidates both
- **B. A complementary reading across two time horizons**
- C. A confirmatory signal
- D. A data error on the platform

Answer: **B**. Different time horizons are not a real conflict; read together they describe short and long term position.

**Q2.** Signals that agree with each other and reinforce the same conclusion are:

- **A. Confirmatory**
- B. Contradictory
- C. Complementary
- D. Contrarian

Answer: **A**. Agreement and reinforcement is the definition of a confirmatory signal.


### Check 15 - Conflicts, and how to settle them

**Q1.** Two identical oscillators on two different platforms give different readings. The most likely reason is:

- A. One platform is fraudulent
- B. Oscillators are random by design
- C. The market was closed
- **D. Differences in data availability, accuracy or type between the platforms**

Answer: **D**. Missing data, or variation in data quality and type between platforms, produces inconsistent readings.

**Q2.** A large bullish ascending triangle contains a smaller bearish complex head and shoulders. Until the neckline breaks, the formation is read as:

- A. Bearish, because the smaller pattern is more recent
- **B. Bullish, because the larger formation takes precedence**
- C. Neutral, because the signals cancel
- D. Unreadable, because the patterns conflict

Answer: **B**. The larger formation carries the longer term sentiment and takes precedence until its own level is breached.


### Check 16 - Bias, entries, and filters

**Q1.** A chartist uses three oscillators and disregards the one that disagrees with their bullish view. This is:

- A. Complementary analysis
- B. A confirmatory signal
- **C. Selective perception**
- D. Mean reversion

Answer: **C**. Heeding only signals that support a preconceived view is selective perception.

**Q2.** Requiring price to close beyond a trendline for a set number of bars before entering is an example of:

- **A. A time filter**
- B. A price filter
- C. An algorithmic filter
- D. A limit entry order

Answer: **A**. A requirement expressed in duration or closed bars is a time-based filter.


## Part 5 of 6 - The Assumptions Underneath Everything

### Check 17 - Discounting

**Q1.** Under the basic assumption of technical analysis, the market cannot discount:

- A. Known information
- B. Expectations about known information
- C. Insider buying and selling activity
- **D. Unexpected events and unknown information**

Answer: **D**. Unexpected events and unknown information are the two things the market cannot discount.

**Q2.** A company reports strong earnings and the share price falls. The best explanation is that:

- A. The market is irrational
- **B. The strong result was already discounted by expectation**
- C. Technical analysis has failed
- D. The earnings report was false

Answer: **B**. Markets discount expectations as well as events, so an anticipated result is already in the price.


### Check 18 - EMH against market discounting

**Q1.** Efficient, under EMH, requires that participants react:

- A. Eventually and profitably
- **B. Instantaneously and rationally**
- C. Collectively and cautiously
- D. Gradually and competitively

Answer: **B**. EMH requires instantaneous and rational reaction to all market information.

**Q2.** The key difference between market discounting in technical analysis and efficient discounting under EMH is that:

- A. Technical analysis requires perfect efficiency, EMH does not
- B. EMH ignores insider activity
- C. There is no real difference between them
- **D. Technical analysis requires only that the market discounts what becomes known to it**

Answer: **D**. Technical analysis imposes no requirement of perfect efficiency, only that everything known is discounted.


### Check 19 - The three forms

**Q1.** Which form of EMH holds that even non-public information is already reflected in price?

- **A. The strong form**
- B. The weak form
- C. The semi-strong form
- D. The random walk form

Answer: **A**. The strong form covers all information, public and private alike.

**Q2.** Select the accurate statements. I. The weak form implies technical analysis is pointless. II. The semi-strong form implies fundamental analysis is pointless. III. The strong form implies all analysis is pointless. IV. The weak form implies insider information is worthless.

- A. Only I and II are correct
- B. Only II and III are correct
- **C. Only I, II, and III are correct**
- D. All are correct

Answer: **C**. Statement IV is false: it is the strong form, not the weak form, that covers non-public information.


### Check 20 - Random walk and real markets

**Q1.** The Markovian condition in random walk states that:

- **A. Current price has no influence on future price**
- B. Past prices determine current price
- C. Prices always revert to a mean
- D. Volume must confirm the trend

Answer: **A**. The Markovian condition is that current price carries no influence over future price.

**Q2.** A stock valued at 10 pesos trades at 30 pesos with no change in fundamentals. This best illustrates that:

- A. The market is inefficient by definition
- **B. Price reflects expectation rather than absolute intrinsic value**
- C. Random walk is correct
- D. The intrinsic value was miscalculated

Answer: **B**. What is traded is expected value, so price and intrinsic value can diverge widely.


### Check 21 - Repetition and trends

**Q1.** The underlying reason market behavior tends to repeat is:

- A. Regulation forces similar outcomes
- B. Prices are mathematically periodic
- **C. Human psychology seldom changes over time**
- D. Charting software enforces patterns

Answer: **C**. Repetition rests on the reliability and consistency of human behavior.

**Q2.** Which is listed as a challenge to trend following?

- A. It cannot be applied to currencies
- B. It requires knowledge of intrinsic value
- C. It only works on daily charts
- **D. Large drawdowns caused by low winning percentages**

Answer: **D**. Low winning percentages and the resulting drawdowns are an explicit challenge to trend following.


### Check 22 - The four applied assumptions

**Q1.** A stochastic reading of 100 percent is explicitly bullish but potentially bearish. This illustrates which applied assumption?

- A. Persistence
- B. Equal and opposite readings
- C. Significance is attributed by participants
- **D. Extreme bullishness is potentially bearish**

Answer: **D**. An extreme reading signals overextension, so extreme bullishness carries a bearish warning.

**Q2.** A poorly designed indicator starts producing reliable signals because a large number of traders risk capital on it. This illustrates:

- A. The weak form of EMH
- B. Random walk
- **C. Applied assumption four, significance attributed by participants**
- D. Mean reversion

Answer: **C**. A tool has only the significance participants give it, which is the self-fulfilling prophecy formalized.


## Part 6 of 6 - Who Is In The Market

### Check 23 - Who is who

**Q1.** A pension fund deploying pooled retirement contributions is best classified as:

- **A. An institutional participant**
- B. A retail participant
- C. A hedger
- D. A novice

Answer: **A**. Institutional participants deploy pooled or corporate capital at scale.

**Q2.** How many main categories of market participant does the chapter identify?

- A. Four
- B. Six
- **C. Eight**
- D. Ten

Answer: **C**. Eight: retail, institutional, speculator, supply side, demand side, professional, investor and novice.


### Check 24 - Categories and methods

**Q1.** A trader who executes a fully coded rule set without overriding it is:

- **A. Nondiscretionary**
- B. Discretionary
- C. A hedger
- D. Supply side

Answer: **A**. Nondiscretionary means following the predefined rules without discretionary intervention.

**Q2.** Which pair is distinguished mainly by who provides and who consumes market services?

- A. Retail and institutional
- **B. Supply side and demand side**
- C. Professional and novice
- D. Speculator and investor

Answer: **B**. Supply side provides access, liquidity and services; demand side consumes them.


### Check 25 - Markets and instruments

**Q1.** Buying shares in a gold backed exchange traded fund is participation in gold through:

- A. The main market
- **B. A derivative**
- C. A hedge
- D. Arbitrage

Answer: **B**. Only buying the physical metal is main market participation; an ETF derives its value from gold.

**Q2.** Select the accurate statements. I. Scalpers are normally in and out within seconds or minutes. II. Position traders hold for a few months to a year. III. Swing traders close all trades within the same day. IV. Investors follow a buy and hold strategy.

- A. Only I and II are correct
- B. Only II, III, and IV are correct
- **C. Only I, II, and IV are correct**
- D. All are correct

Answer: **C**. Statement III describes day traders; swing traders hold for a few days to a week.
