## Part 1 of 6 - Where Dow Theory Came From

### Check 1 - The four proponents

**Q1.** Which proponent was responsible for much of the categorizing, refining and formal codification of Dow's basic premises, later laid out in his book The Dow Theory?

- A. William P. Hamilton
- B. Charles H. Dow
- **C. Robert Rhea**
- D. S. A. Nelson

Answer: **C**. Rhea, a student of Hamilton, did the codification and wrote The Dow Theory.

**Q2.** Who was the first person to refer to Dow's concepts and ideas as the Dow Theory?

- **A. S. A. Nelson**
- B. Robert Rhea
- C. Charles H. Dow
- D. William P. Hamilton

Answer: **A**. Nelson, a close acquaintance, named it in The ABC of Stock Speculation.


### Check 2 - Rhea's assumptions

**Q1.** One of Rhea's basic assumptions was:

- A. The averages do not discount everything
- B. That Dow Theory itself is perfect
- C. That the markets are efficient
- **D. The primary trend is not susceptible to manipulation**

Answer: **D**. Rhea's first assumption. Manipulation is possible only over the shorter term.

**Q2.** According to Rhea, Dow Theory should at most be regarded as:

- A. A guaranteed method of making a profit
- **B. A set of guidelines for investing**
- C. A mathematical model of price
- D. A replacement for fundamental analysis

Answer: **B**. Rhea's third assumption says the theory is not perfect and guarantees nothing.


### Check 3 - The averages

**Q1.** Dow published his first stock market average in 1884. How many stocks were in it?

- **A. Eleven**
- B. Twelve
- C. Twenty
- D. Thirty

Answer: **A**. Eleven in 1884. Twelve industrials and twenty railroads came later, and thirty is the modern industrial count.

**Q2.** The Railroad Average is known today as:

- A. The 12 stock Industrial Index
- B. The Dow Jones Industrial Average
- **C. The Dow Jones Transportation Average**
- D. The 11 stock average of 1884

Answer: **C**. The Railroad Average became the Dow Jones Transportation Average.


## Part 2 of 6 - The Six Tenets, and the First One

### Check 4 - The six tenets

**Q1.** Pick the statements below that represent the tenets of Dow Theory. I. The averages discount everything. II. The market has three trends. III. All trends have three phases. IV. Volume must confirm the trend.

- A. Only I and III
- B. Only III and IV
- **C. Only I, II and IV**
- D. All are correct

Answer: **C**. Statement III overstates it. The tenet is that primary trends have three phases, not all trends.

**Q2.** In addition to the six basic tenets, Dow Theory recognizes:

- A. Only opening prices
- B. Only the day's high and low
- C. Both opening and closing prices
- **D. Only closing prices**

Answer: **D**. The book states it separately from the six: only closing prices are recognized.


### Check 5 - Discounting

**Q1.** In Dow Theory, the market discounts everything except:

- A. Company earnings
- **B. Acts of God**
- C. Interest rate changes
- D. Government policy

Answer: **B**. Only unexpected events and unknown information escape it, and even those are absorbed quickly.

**Q2.** As far as Dow Theory is concerned:

- A. The market must discount everything instantaneously
- B. The participants must be rational
- **C. It is not infallible**
- D. All participants must act on all information

Answer: **C**. Rhea's third assumption. The other three are precisely the requirements Dow Theory does not make.


### Check 6 - Closing prices

**Q1.** A share trades between 96 and 104 pesos during the day and closes at 100. What does Dow Theory record?

- A. The whole range, 96 to 104
- B. The high and the low, 104 and 96
- C. The average of the high and the low
- **D. The close, 100**

Answer: **D**. Only the final closing price is used, however large the day's excursion was.

**Q2.** Under Dow Theory, a close one centavo above the previous close is:

- A. Too small to count
- **B. A higher close**
- C. Counted only if volume expands
- D. Counted only on a weekly chart

Answer: **B**. It does not matter how miniscule the amount above or below the previous close is.


## Part 3 of 6 - Three Trends, and the Primary One

### Check 7 - The three trends

**Q1.** In Dow Theory, the three trends:

- A. Take turns, one at a time
- **B. May all be in progress at one and the same time**
- C. Are all of equal importance
- D. Appear only on weekly charts

Answer: **B**. Rhea's own words. All three movements may be running together.

**Q2.** Which trend does Dow Theory call the most deceptive?

- A. The primary trend
- B. The minor trend
- **C. The secondary reaction**
- D. None of them is called deceptive

Answer: **C**. Rhea calls the secondary reaction the second and most deceptive movement.


### Check 8 - Uptrend and downtrend

**Q1.** In Dow Theory, an uptrend is defined primarily as:

- A. A rise of more than twenty percent
- B. A close above the previous close for three days
- **C. Successively higher peaks and troughs**
- D. Any advance lasting more than three weeks

Answer: **C**. The definition is the sequence of peaks and troughs, not a size or a duration.

**Q2.** An uptrend has run 104, 98, 116, 108, 128, 119, 141, 128, and now makes a peak at 134. What has just happened?

- A. The trend has made another higher peak
- B. The trend has made a lower trough
- C. Nothing Dow Theory takes any notice of
- **D. The first lower peak has appeared**

Answer: **D**. 134 is below the previous peak of 141, so the uptrend has technically ended.


### Check 9 - Signals on the primary trend

**Q1.** In Dow Theory, buy and sell signals are indicated by:

- **A. The penetration of a previous peak or trough in the primary trend**
- B. Retracements exceeding twenty percent of the primary trend's range
- C. The primary trend lasting more than one year
- D. A close above the day's opening price

Answer: **A**. Penetration of a previous peak or trough is the signal, and it is judged on the close.

**Q2.** Critics say Dow Theory's signals usually miss:

- A. The whole of the trend
- **B. One third or more of the entire trend**
- C. Only the first week of the trend
- D. Nothing that can be measured

Answer: **B**. Waiting for confirmation costs one third or more of the move, which Dow accepted.


### Check 10 - Scaling

**Q1.** Logarithmically scaled charts tend to give:

- **A. Earlier trend change signals**
- B. Later trend change signals
- C. Exactly the same signals as arithmetic charts
- D. No trend change signals at all

Answer: **A**. Uptrend lines are violated sooner on a log scale, so the signal comes earlier.

**Q2.** On a logarithmic price axis, equal vertical distances represent:

- A. Equal amounts of pesos
- B. Equal numbers of trading days
- **C. Equal percentage moves**
- D. Equal volumes traded

Answer: **C**. Log scaling is proportional, so 100 to 110 and 200 to 220 are the same height.


## Part 4 of 6 - The Secondary Reaction and the Minor Trend

### Check 11 - How deep a reaction goes

**Q1.** In Dow Theory, the secondary trend:

- A. Moves in the direction of the primary trend
- B. Lasts from months to years
- **C. Normally retraces from one third to two thirds of the primary trend's range**
- D. Is always accompanied by very low volume

Answer: **C**. It moves against the primary trend, lasts weeks to about three months, and retraces one to two thirds.

**Q2.** A retracement that goes beyond two thirds on high volume usually signifies:

- A. A deeper than usual secondary reaction
- B. That the minor trend has taken over
- C. That volume has stopped confirming
- **D. That it may in fact be a new primary bear market**

Answer: **D**. Past two thirds on high volume, the book stops calling it a reaction.


### Check 12 - Resumption

**Q1.** A primary bull trend resumes its uptrend once price:

- **A. Breaches the highest peak formed by the secondary reaction**
- B. Falls back by less than half of the primary trend's range
- C. Closes above its own opening price
- D. Retraces beyond two thirds

Answer: **A**. The reaction ends when the peak it formed is penetrated.

**Q2.** Figure 2.12 shows a reaction of about 75 percent that was still a secondary reaction. What does that tell you?

- A. The one third to two thirds guide is a rule with no exceptions
- **B. Depth alone does not settle what a move is**
- C. Retracements are always measured on weekly charts
- D. The primary trend had already ended

Answer: **B**. It is why Rhea called the secondary reaction the most deceptive movement.


### Check 13 - Minor trends and lines

**Q1.** Which of the following did Dow consider tradable? I. Primary trend. II. Secondary trend. III. Minor trends. IV. Lines in the market.

- **A. Only I and IV**
- B. Only I, II and III
- C. Only I
- D. All are correct

Answer: **A**. The primary trend, and lines. Nothing else is tradable under Dow Theory.

**Q2.** A line is best described as:

- A. A trendline drawn through the lows
- B. The direction of the primary trend
- **C. A narrow horizontal ranging formation on the daily chart**
- D. The 50 percent retracement level

Answer: **C**. A line is flat price action, not a line anybody draws on the chart.


## Part 5 of 6 - Three Phases, and When a Trend Reverses

### Check 14 - The three phases

**Q1.** Under Dow Theory, three phases belong to:

- A. All trends
- B. Secondary reactions only
- C. Minor trends only
- **D. Primary trends**

Answer: **D**. The tenet is that primary trends have three phases. Any wider claim is wrong.

**Q2.** The accumulation phase normally lasts longer than the distribution phase because:

- **A. There is less capital and profit at risk at the lower prices**
- B. Informed buyers are slower than informed sellers
- C. Accumulation always follows a bear market
- D. Volume is lower at the bottom of a market

Answer: **A**. Distribution happens at the top, where much more capital and unrealized profit is at risk.


### Check 15 - Who is buying, and when

**Q1.** During accumulation, the uninformed market participants are usually:

- A. Extremely optimistic and buying at any price
- **B. Extremely bearish and selling at any price available**
- C. Absent from the market entirely
- D. Buying on margin

Answer: **B**. Accumulation follows very negative data, so the uninformed are selling into it.

**Q2.** The state in which uninformed participants buy up whatever shares are available at any price is normally referred to as:

- A. Market noise
- B. Non confirmation
- **C. Irrational exuberance**
- D. A secondary reaction

Answer: **C**. The book uses the phrase for the crowd at the top, during distribution.


### Check 16 - Reversal formations

**Q1.** In Dow Theory, a trend is assumed to persist:

- A. For at least three months
- **B. Until there is evidence to the contrary**
- C. Until volume stops expanding
- D. Until the averages disagree

Answer: **B**. Trend changes are identified by penetration of a previous significant peak or trough.

**Q2.** A top reversal in which the second peak succeeds in penetrating the previous peak is:

- A. A failure swing
- B. A double top
- **C. A non failure swing**
- D. A secondary reaction

Answer: **C**. Fails to reach it is a failure swing, matches it is a double top, exceeds it is a non failure swing.


### Check 17 - Where the signal falls

**Q1.** The conclusive sell signal on a non failure swing comes at the penetration of:

- A. The first and higher support level
- **B. The second and lower support level**
- C. The previous peak
- D. The 50 percent retracement level

Answer: **B**. The formation was still making a higher peak, so more evidence is required.

**Q2.** A top reversal in which the second peak matches the level of the previous peak is:

- **A. A double top**
- B. A failure swing
- C. A non failure swing
- D. A line

Answer: **A**. Matching the previous peak is the double top, the middle of the three variations.


## Part 6 of 6 - Confirmation, Volume, and the Challenges

### Check 18 - Confirmation

**Q1.** Confirmation of the averages means that:

- A. Both averages must rise by more than twenty percent
- **B. Both the Industrials Average and the Railroad Average must extend beyond their secondary peaks**
- C. Both averages must close higher on the same day
- D. Both averages must breach their minor peaks

Answer: **B**. Secondary peaks, and the trend in one average must be confirmed by the other.

**Q2.** One average penetrates its secondary peak at T1 and the other penetrates its own at T2, which is later. When is the uptrend confirmed?

- A. At T1
- B. Halfway between T1 and T2
- **C. At T2**
- D. It is never confirmed

Answer: **C**. The later average dates the signal. Until then the move is unconfirmed.


### Check 19 - Volume

**Q1.** In Dow Theory, volume confirmation of the existing trend implies that:

- **A. In an uptrend, volume should be increasing**
- B. In an uptrend, volume should be increasing during a downside retracement
- C. In a downtrend, volume should be decreasing
- D. Volume should increase during accumulation and decrease during distribution

Answer: **A**. Volume expands with the trend and contracts against it. The other three reverse that.

**Q2.** If volume does not expand in the direction of the existing trend, the book says this is:

- A. Proof that the trend has reversed
- **B. A sign of weakness that may lead to a reversal**
- C. Irrelevant, since volume is a secondary indicator
- D. A confirmation of the secondary reaction

Answer: **B**. A sign of weakness, and only potentially a reversal. The book hedges and so should you.


### Check 20 - The challenges

**Q1.** Which criticism attacks Rhea's first assumption directly?

- A. Only closing prices are recognized
- B. The signals arrive late in the trend
- **C. The primary trend is susceptible to manipulation**
- D. Dow Theory suits the equity markets best

Answer: **C**. Rhea assumed the primary trend could not be manipulated. The book now says it can.

**Q2.** Why does the book say confirmation between the Industrials and the Transports has become less effective?

- A. The two averages now hold the same companies
- **B. Many industrial companies no longer produce anything that needs shipping**
- C. The Transportation Average is no longer published
- D. Transport costs are no longer reported

Answer: **B**. The original logic was industry producing goods and transport shipping them.


### Check 21 - What survives

**Q1.** The book's own summary says Dow Theory is still widely regarded as reliable for:

- A. Forecasting the exact extent of a primary trend
- B. Timing entries on the minor trend
- C. Pricing derivatives on the averages
- **D. Determining and confirming that a trend exists**

Answer: **D**. Determining and confirming the existence of a trend, which is the whole claim.

**Q2.** A commodity trader following Dow Theory strictly would most likely complain that:

- A. The theory ignores volume
- **B. A signal on the primary trend can take months or years to arrive**
- C. The theory has too many tenets to apply
- D. Closing prices are unavailable in commodities

Answer: **B**. The first criticism: the theory suits equities, and slow signals make hedging impractical.
