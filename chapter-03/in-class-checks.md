## Part 1 of 6 - From Price to OHLC

### Check 1 - Chartists and the time axis

**Q1.** Analysts who use charts to study technical data are called:

- A. Quantitative analysts
- B. Statisticians
- **C. Chartists**
- D. Arbitrageurs

Answer: **C**. The book's own word. Quants may use charts too, but for them it is optional.

**Q2.** On a chart where the time axis acts only as a counter for new blocks of data, time is:

- A. Plotted in equal units of time
- **B. Regarded as implicit along the x axis**
- C. Plotted on the vertical axis
- D. Left off the chart altogether

Answer: **B**. The time axis still runs left to right, but it no longer measures equal time.


### Check 2 - Quantization and OHLC

**Q1.** In one five minute interval a share opens at 50.00, trades as high as 50.80 and as low as 49.60, and ends at 50.40. What is its closing price?

- A. 50.00
- B. 50.80
- C. 49.60
- **D. 50.40**

Answer: **D**. The close is the price at the end of the interval. The options are listed in the order O, H, L, C.

**Q2.** A bar has a high of 50.80 and a low of 49.60. Its range is:

- A. 0.40
- B. 0.80
- **C. 1.20**
- D. 1.60

Answer: **C**. Range is the absolute difference between the high and the low, 50.80 minus 49.60.


### Check 3 - Higher timeframes and auto-scaling

**Q1.** To build one 15 minute bar out of three 5 minute bars, its opening price is:

- **A. The open of the first 5 minute bar**
- B. The open of the last 5 minute bar
- C. The highest of the three opens
- D. The average of the three opens

Answer: **A**. Open of the first period, close of the last, and the highest high and lowest low between them.

**Q2.** According to the book, leaving auto-scaling switched on:

- A. Preserves the vertical scale
- B. Is needed to see a gap
- C. Affects only line charts
- **D. Misrepresents volatility on the charts**

Answer: **D**. Flat prices are stretched to look volatile and trending prices are squeezed to look calm.


## Part 2 of 6 - What the Four Prices Mean, and Four Kinds of Gap

### Check 4 - Significance of OHLC

**Q1.** According to the book, the opening and closing prices are:

- A. Created by the forces of supply and demand
- **B. Merely a function of time stamping**
- C. The most significant prices in a bar
- D. Areas of price rejection

Answer: **B**. They mark where an arbitrary interval started and stopped. The high and low are set by traders.

**Q2.** In which market does the book say the opening and closing prices matter less than the high and low?

- **A. Spot foreign exchange**
- B. A stock exchange with a lunch break
- C. Any market on a weekly chart
- D. Futures near expiry

Answer: **A**. It trades continuously through the week, so there is no long break to give an open weight.


### Check 5 - The four gaps

**Q1.** The last bar had a high of 104 and closed at 102. The new bar opens at 106. What is the Type 1 gap?

- A. 1.50 pesos
- B. 2 pesos
- C. 3.50 pesos
- **D. 4 pesos**

Answer: **D**. Type 1 is measured from the previous close to the new open: 106 minus 102.

**Q2.** Which gap is usually referred to as a window in Japanese candlestick and bar charts?

- A. Type 1
- B. Type 2
- **C. Type 3**
- D. Type 4

Answer: **C**. Type 3, measured from the previous bar's high or low to the current bar's low or high.


### Check 6 - What gaps do

**Q1.** Once a gap has formed, the book says prices are generally expected to:

- A. Never return to it
- **B. Return to fill it later, although often they do not**
- C. Fill it before the same bar closes
- D. Reverse the trend immediately

Answer: **B**. The book expects a gap to fill and says in the same sentence that many do not.

**Q2.** The last close was 102. The new bar has a high of 108 and a low of 105.50. What is its true range?

- A. 2.50 pesos
- B. 3.50 pesos
- **C. 6 pesos**
- D. 8.50 pesos

Answer: **C**. The greater of the range, 2.50, and the distance from the last close to the new high, 6.


## Part 3 of 6 - Five Constant Measures

### Check 7 - Constant time, bar and line charts

**Q1.** On a constant time chart, which of these is the same for every bar?

- A. The range
- **B. The duration**
- C. The volume
- D. The volatility

Answer: **B**. Each bar closes when the set interval of time has elapsed. Everything else varies.

**Q2.** Compared with a bar chart, a line chart:

- A. Shows more about supply and demand
- B. Needs volume data to construct
- **C. Filters out more of the price information**
- D. Records the high and low of every bar

Answer: **C**. It keeps only the closes and ignores the highs and lows, the true indicators of market force.


### Check 8 - Candlesticks and equivolume

**Q1.** The space between the opening and closing price of a candlestick is called:

- **A. The real body**
- B. The shadow
- C. The range
- D. The wick

Answer: **A**. The body is boxed up between the open and the close. Shadows run out to the high and low.

**Q2.** Which of these is true of an equivolume chart?

- A. Volume is shown as bar height
- B. It is not a constant time chart
- C. Its bars are built from the open and the close
- **D. Its time axis is not plotted in a linear fashion**

Answer: **D**. Each bar covers one interval, but the varying widths make the time axis non linear.


### Check 9 - Point and figure and Renko

**Q1.** A point and figure chart uses a 1 peso box and a 3 box reversal. How far must price move against the current column before a reversal is plotted?

- A. 1 peso
- B. 2 pesos
- **C. 3 pesos**
- D. 4 pesos

Answer: **C**. Three boxes of 1 peso each.

**Q2.** Renko charts are essentially:

- A. One brick reversal charts
- **B. Two brick reversal charts**
- C. Three line break charts
- D. Constant time charts

Answer: **B**. All Renko reversals need price to move at least two bricks the other way.


### Check 10 - Volume, tick and volatility charts

**Q1.** Which chart plots a new bar once a set number of transactions has taken place?

- A. A constant volume chart
- B. A constant range chart
- C. A constant volatility chart
- **D. A constant transaction chart**

Answer: **D**. Constant transaction, or tick, charts count trades, whatever the size of each one.

**Q2.** A constant volatility chart sets each bar at 2 x ATR. The ATR is 0.50 pesos. How far must price move to complete a bar?

- A. 0.25 peso
- B. 0.50 peso
- **C. 1 peso**
- D. 2 pesos

Answer: **C**. Two multiples of an ATR of 0.50 pesos.


### Check 11 - Which measure is constant

**Q1.** Which chart possesses no measures of constancy at all?

- A. A Renko chart
- **B. A three line break chart**
- C. A tick chart
- D. An equivolume chart

Answer: **B**. Range, duration, volume, transactions and volatility are all variable on a three line break chart.

**Q2.** On which of these is the time axis plotted in a non linear fashion? I. Bar chart. II. Point and figure chart. III. Line chart. IV. Constant volume chart.

- A. Only I and III
- **B. Only II and IV**
- C. Only IV
- D. All are correct

Answer: **B**. Bar and line charts are constant time with a linear axis. The other two do not run on time.


## Part 4 of 6 - Chart Scaling

### Check 12 - Linear and ratio

**Q1.** On a linear scale, a rise from 10 to 20 pesos and a rise from 90 to 100 pesos:

- **A. Are drawn the same height**
- B. Represent the same percentage change
- C. Cannot be shown on one chart
- D. Are drawn the same height only on a weekly chart

Answer: **A**. Equal price changes, equal distances. Their percentages are 100 and 11.1.

**Q2.** On a ratio scale, a rise from 10 to 20 pesos is drawn the same height as a rise from 90 to:

- A. 100 pesos
- B. 110 pesos
- C. 135 pesos
- **D. 180 pesos**

Answer: **D**. Both are 100 percent rises, and equal percentage changes are equal distances on a ratio scale.


### Check 13 - Choosing a scale

**Q1.** According to the book, ratio scaling provides:

- A. Better definition at the upper end of prices
- B. Equal definition across the whole range
- **C. Better definition at the lower end of prices**
- D. No definition of volatility

Answer: **C**. It compresses higher prices and expands lower ones, so the lower end is clearer.

**Q2.** Which scale does the book recommend for futures and highly leveraged foreign exchange?

- **A. Linear**
- B. Ratio
- C. Square root
- D. Logarithmic

Answer: **A**. Ratio would compress price too much at higher prices in markets traded on very low margin.


### Check 14 - Overlays and trendlines

**Q1.** Switching a chart from linear to ratio scaling will change the reading of:

- A. A moving average
- B. A prior support level
- **C. A trendline**
- D. A Fibonacci retracement

Answer: **C**. A trendline is geometric. Moving averages are numerical and the other two are horizontal.

**Q2.** Which pair describes a linear scaled chart?

- A. Uptrend lines penetrated sooner, downtrend lines later
- **B. Uptrend lines penetrated later, downtrend lines sooner**
- C. Both penetrated sooner
- D. Both penetrated later

Answer: **B**. The reverse of the ratio chart, where uptrend lines break sooner.


### Check 15 - Appearances

**Q1.** On a ratio scaled chart, rising prices may take on:

- **A. A bearish appearance, decelerating like a rounding top**
- B. A bullish appearance, accelerating like a parabolic move
- C. A bullish appearance, rising evenly
- D. No change in appearance

Answer: **A**. Ratio charts compress higher prices, so a rise decelerates in a concave curve.

**Q2.** On a linear scaled chart, falling prices may take on:

- A. A bearish appearance, accelerating like a parabolic move
- B. A bearish appearance, like a rounding top
- C. No change in appearance
- **D. A bullish appearance, decelerating like a rounding bottom**

Answer: **D**. Linear makes a decline decelerate in a concave curve, which looks like a rounding bottom.


## Part 5 of 6 - The Bid-Ask Spread

### Check 16 - Expensive and early longs

**Q1.** The spread is 20 centavos. You buy at market the moment the bid chart touches 10.05. You are most likely filled at:

- A. 9.85
- B. 10.05
- **C. 10.25**
- D. 10.45

Answer: **C**. A buy accepts the ask, 20 centavos above the bid. This is the expensive longs.

**Q2.** A buy stop at 10.05 fills at 10.05 while the bid is 9.85, below the 10.00 breakout. The book calls this the problem of:

- A. The late longs
- **B. The early longs**
- C. The expensive longs
- D. Negative slippage

Answer: **B**. Filled at the intended price, but before the breakout on the bid chart.


### Check 17 - Buying at support

**Q1.** A pending order to buy at a 10.00 support level fills at 10.00 while the bid is already 9.80. This is the problem of:

- **A. The late longs**
- B. The early longs
- C. The expensive longs
- D. Positive slippage

Answer: **A**. The order filled at the intended price, but only after support had broken on the bid chart.

**Q2.** With a 20 centavo spread, you buy at market as the bid chart tests support at 10.00. You are filled at:

- A. 9.80
- B. 10.00
- C. 10.10
- **D. 10.20**

Answer: **D**. A market buy accepts the ask, 20 centavos above the bid.


### Check 18 - The spread and R/r

**Q1.** You buy at market when the bid is 1.00 and the spread is 0.20, with a target of 1.50 and a stop of 0.50. The spread adjusted reward to risk ratio is:

- A. 0.30
- **B. 0.43**
- C. 0.70
- D. 1.00

Answer: **B**. Bought at 1.20: a profit of 0.30 against a loss of 0.70, and 0.30 over 0.70 is 0.43.

**Q2.** The book's most obvious way to reduce the relative cost of the spread is to:

- **A. Trade a higher timeframe or a larger wave cycle**
- B. Use market orders only
- C. Trade a lower timeframe
- D. Switch to a line chart

Answer: **A**. The larger the target and stop, the smaller the spread is relative to them.


## Part 6 of 6 - Futures Contracts

### Check 19 - Liquidity and the front month

**Q1.** Volume and open interest in a futures contract are normally greatest:

- A. In its first month of trading
- **B. About two to three months before expiry**
- C. In its last week before expiry
- D. On the expiry day itself

Answer: **B**. Volume subsides nearer expiry as traders roll into the next contract.

**Q2.** The front month contract has:

- A. The widest bid-ask spread
- B. The largest spread with respect to the spot price
- **C. The expiration date closest to the current date**
- D. The least liquidity of all the contracts

Answer: **C**. It also has the narrowest spreads and is the most liquid. The other three are reversed.


### Check 20 - Roll yields and contango

**Q1.** A long holder who can profit in a declining or sideways market is one whose rollovers are at:

- A. A premium large enough
- **B. A discount large enough**
- C. The spot price exactly
- D. The front month price exactly

Answer: **B**. Rolling at a discount pays the trader, a positive roll yield, at every rollover.

**Q2.** Further out contracts trade at 99, 98, 97 and 96 against an expected spot price of 100 at expiry. The market is in:

- A. Normal contango
- B. Simple contango
- **C. Normal backwardation**
- D. Negative roll yield

Answer: **C**. Successively lower than the expected spot price at expiry is normal backwardation.


### Check 21 - Convergence

**Q1.** At expiry, a futures price:

- **A. Always converges to the spot price**
- B. Always stays above the spot price
- C. Always stays below the spot price
- D. Converges only in backwardation

Answer: **A**. Convergence at expiry holds for every contract, in contango or in backwardation.

**Q2.** With the spot price constant, a contract in contango will:

- A. Rise toward the spot, with positive yields
- B. Stay where it is until expiry
- C. Rise, with negative yields
- **D. Decline toward the spot, with negative yields**

Answer: **D**. It trades above the spot and must converge down to it by expiry.


### Check 22 - Unadjusted and back adjusted

**Q1.** In a back-adjusted continuous chart:

- A. Historical prices remain unchanged
- **B. The current market price is always displayed**
- C. There are gaps at every rollover
- D. Back testing is impossible

Answer: **B**. The history is shifted to meet the current contract, so today's price is the real one.

**Q2.** An unadjusted nearest futures chart:

- A. Removes the gaps between contracts
- B. Always displays an estimated price
- C. Is best for back testing
- **D. Is an accurate record of historical prices**

Answer: **D**. It keeps the actual nearby prices, gaps and all, which is also why analysis on it fails.


### Check 23 - Perpetual contracts

**Q1.** Perpetual contracts:

- A. Display the current market price
- B. Keep changing historical prices at each roll
- **C. Display an estimated price, which is useless for trading**
- D. Contain a gap at every rollover

Answer: **C**. The price is interpolated between expiries by a weighting factor, so nobody traded at it.

**Q2.** For relative strength charts the book prefers:

- **A. Perpetual contracts**
- B. Back-adjusted continuous charts
- C. Unadjusted nearest futures charts
- D. Tick charts

Answer: **A**. Their history is not back shifted, so the ratio between two series is not distorted.
